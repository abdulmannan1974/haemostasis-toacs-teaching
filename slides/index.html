Pre-operative coagulation screening is reported as abnormal and surgery is postponed. The biomedical scientist notes that the citrate tube was visibly under-filled. His haematocrit (the fraction of the blood made up of red cells) is 0.62. His first clotting screen looked abnormal, so surgery was postponed. The laboratory then noticed the citrate tube was under-filled, and a repeat sample taken into a tube with less citrate was normal.

Explain the mechanism by which each of these prolongs a clotting time.

Answer

  • Citrate 1:9 with blood; chelates ionised calcium
  • Under-fill: same citrate, less blood
  • High haematocrit: same blood, less plasma
  • Added calcium only partly repletes
  • PT and APTT long; TT spared

Station 02 · 4 marks
The titre is 12 NBU/mL, yet 3 IU/dL of factor VIII is still measurable. Explain this using inhibitor kinetics. In what circumstance would the plasma need heat treatment at 56 °C before the Bethesda assay, and why?

Answer

  • Type II kinetics: fast, then slow, never complete
  • Residual factor VIII coexists with a high titre
  • Type I: second-order, complete — congenital alloantibodies
  • Heat 56 °C, 30 min, centrifuge: removes factor VIII
  • Needed whenever FVIII is present: at presentation and during replacement
RememberType I takes it all; type II always leaves some behind.
Common mistakeAssuming that measurable factor VIII means a weak inhibitor, or applying type I (complete inactivation) kinetics to acquired haemophilia.
